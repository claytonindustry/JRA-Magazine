How much can be marked at once

The last two pages marked a single word and it was protected every time. The sentence that failed in issue 300 was not a single word — it was a whole sentence among four. This changes only one thing down the list: how much of the paragraph is wrapped.

How to run it
  1. Right-click the page, choose Translate to…, French.
  2. Read down the six paragraphs. Each one says underneath it which part was meant to be protected.
  3. Tell me which numbers kept their protected part in English.

Row 1 is the exact markup from issue 300 that failed. If it fails here too, we have it cornered.

6 · the paragraph element itself marked — the one we know works

6.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that easing a sail was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

↑ protected: all of it, by the paragraph

What the answer would mean

1 fails and 2 holds — it is the size of the marked run. Chrome protects an island inside a sentence but loses a whole sentence marked among others. That is a rule we can work with: protect words and phrases, never whole sentences.

1 and 3 both hold — the markup is fine in every shape, and something about the magazine itself is the cause after all, not the content. I would want to see the real page failing before building anything else.

Only 5 and 6 hold — nothing short of the whole paragraph is safe, and the control should mark blocks rather than phrases.
