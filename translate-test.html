Words that arrive late

The magazine does not have its articles when the page opens — it fetches them and writes them in a moment later. This asks whether that is what breaks the protection, by showing the same four sentences twice: once sitting in the page from the start, and once arriving after you have already translated.

How to run it — the order matters
  1. Right-click the page and choose Translate to… French. Wait for part one to turn French.
  2. Read part one and note which lines still say lighthouse.
  3. Now press the button in part two. Four more sentences appear.
  4. Read those and note which of them still say lighthouse.
  5. Tell me both lists. If the two differ, arriving late is the whole problem and the markup was never at fault.

What the answer would mean

Both parts behave the same — arriving late is not the problem, and I am still looking in the wrong place.

Part one protected, part two translated — confirmed. Chrome reads the opt-out when it first walks the page and forgets it for anything written in afterwards. The markup in the magazine is correct and always was.

Part two protected only on the paragraph, or only in the shadow root — that tells us which of the two is worth building, which is the whole point of asking.
