One word in a real paragraph

We know now that nothing marked inside a paragraph of real prose survives: a word, a clause, a sentence, two sentences — all translated. Only wrapping the whole paragraph works. This asks whether there is any way at all to keep one word, using methods that do not rely on Chrome honouring a mark.

How to run it
  1. Right-click the page, choose Translate to…, French.
  2. Each paragraph below should contain the English word lighthouse where the French would be phare.
  3. Tell me which numbers still say lighthouse.

Ignore the small grey captions — they are outside everything and are meant to turn French. That is how you know it worked.

Five ways to keep one word

1 · nothing — the control, must turn French

1.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that rowing past the lighthouse was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

2 · a span with both opt-outs — known to fail, here for comparison

2.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that rowing past the lighthouse was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

3 · the word in an open shadow root — not in the page's own tree

3.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that rowing past the lighthouse was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

4 · the word drawn by the stylesheet, not written in the page

4.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that rowing past the was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

5 · the whole paragraph marked — the one we know works

5.He accused other sailors of being lazy. He declared that rowing past the lighthouse was a sign of weakness. He insisted that anyone who wasn't pulling every rope as tightly as possible had no business being on the water. His crew grew increasingly exhausted.

What the answer would mean

3 keeps its word — a boat's name can be protected mid-sentence after all. The word stays real text you can select, copy and search; it simply lives one step to the side, where the translator does not look. This is the answer you asked for and I would build it.

Only 4 keeps its word — possible, but I would not recommend it. The word would not be selectable, not findable with Ctrl+F, and awkward for anyone reading with their ears. A last resort.

Only 5 — then it is settled: whole paragraphs are the only thing Chrome will leave alone, the mark becomes a block-level control, and protecting a boat's name mid-sentence needs the magazine to own its own translation.
